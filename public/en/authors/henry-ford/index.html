Henry Ford

The factory as an argument.

Between workbench and world market: the story of a man whose ideas shaped the working life of the twentieth century — for better and for worse.

01

Life & period

Ford was born on a Michigan farm in 1863 and came to automobile building by way of the machine shop. He founded the Ford Motor Company in 1903.

With the Model T of 1908, the moving assembly line of 1913, and the five-dollar day of 1914, he changed manufacturing, wages, and daily life alike.

02

Method

Ford's prose does not narrate; it argues. He reasons with unit counts, wages, prices, and hand movements, and the figures are the argument rather than its decoration.

His sentences are strikingly short and aphoristic — a manner of writing that mirrors his own doctrine of efficiency.

03

Influence and contradiction

Ford's manufacturing idea was copied worldwide and still bears his name. At the same time the newspaper he published, The Dearborn Independent, circulated antisemitic tracts during the 1920s.

Aeternus does not smooth this contradiction away. The source text stands unaltered; contextualization is kept visibly separate, in the publisher's apparatus.
